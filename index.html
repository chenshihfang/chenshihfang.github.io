Doctoral Research

Human visual perception lies in the ability to maintain a continuous and coherent understanding of the external world by integrating observations with accumulated experience. The human visual system continuously adapts to changes in targets and environments by leveraging prior knowledge, spatial geometry, and semantic context. As a core problem in computer vision, visual object tracking aims to bring machine perception closer to human visual perception.

Browse slides

Generic Object Tracking (GOT) aims to continuously localize an arbitrarily specified target in dynamic visual streams. However, unpredictable target and environmental variations make generalization and test-time adaptation key bottlenecks, particularly under severe deformation, complex distractors, environmental changes, and unseen target categories.

This dissertation aims to narrow the gap between machine visual tracking and human visual perception by proposing a series of ML methods that systematically enhance target discrimination, robust adaptation, and geometric reasoning capabilities.
